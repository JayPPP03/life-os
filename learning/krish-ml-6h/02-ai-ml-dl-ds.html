📋 Coach's Review — common learner gaps in this topic

You haven't written notes for this topic yet. When you do, the coach checks them against this list — the things learners most often miss or get wrong here:

① Using AI and ML as synonyms.
The expected answer is containment — DL ⊂ ML ⊂ AI, said in symbols first, then one example per layer. Interchangeable terms = instant credibility hit in interviews.
② Calling an XGBoost/boosted-trees model 'deep learning'.
No neural network → not DL. Boosted trees are classical ML. (Your MyOperator churn model is ML in production — say it at the right level.)
③ Defining DL by 'it's the new/advanced stuff'.
The definition is by MECHANISM: multi-layered neural networks mimicking the brain, happiest on big data + GPUs. 'Advanced' is not a mechanism.
④ Assuming DL is always the better choice.
On tabular business data (churn, forecasts, recommenders), classical ML usually wins and is explainable. DL earns its keep on perception: faces, voice, images.
⑤ Putting Data Science inside the Venn as a fourth subset.
DS is the practitioner discipline working ACROSS the stack — collection, cleaning, EDA, modeling (ML or DL), business communication — not a sibling box.
⑥ Mixing levels mid-answer.
'Tesla is AI, my recommender is ML, face unlock is DL…' without the containment sentence first leaves the interviewer doing the organizing for you. Symbols → examples, in that order.

📝 Notes

This section draws the family tree that every interviewer expects you to draw cleanly.

  • Artificial Intelligence (AI) — the umbrella: applications that perform tasks without human intervention. Everyday examples given: Netflix recommendations, Amazon product suggestions, online ads, Tesla self-driving.
  • Machine Learning (ML) — a subset of AI. Provides statistical tools to analyze, visualize, predict and forecast from data. Examples: forecasting weather/sales, recommendation systems, image and speech analysis.
  • Deep Learning (DL) — a subset of ML that mimics the human brain using multi-layered neural networks. Runs best on big data + GPUs. Examples: face recognition, self-driving perception, voice assistants.
  • Data Science — the practitioner discipline working across all of these: data collection, cleaning, EDA, ML/DL modeling, and business communication. A Data Scientist may touch AI, ML and DL in the same quarter.
AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL   |   Data Science = practice across the stack

💡 Interview angle: the classic trap is mixing the levels. Say it as containment: 'DL ⊂ ML ⊂ AI', then one crisp example per layer. Job titles align to layers: AI engineer, ML engineer/developer, DL engineer, Data Scientist.

🎯 Prove it with your projects

  • Your Project-01 recommender is a classic ML system: statistical tools (SVD matrix factorization, TF-IDF) on interaction data — no neural networks needed, and that was a deliberate sizing decision.
  • Your churn model at MyOperator: ML in production — XGBoost + MLOps (MLflow, CI/CD on SageMaker), the 'statistical tools' layer delivering business value.
  • Say it in interviews: 'I work in the ML layer of AI — and I size the tool to the problem; I chose matrix factorization over deep learning because it was right for the data scale.'
