📋 Coach's Review — common learner gaps in this topic

You haven't written notes for this topic yet. When you do, the coach checks them against this list — the things learners most often miss or get wrong here:

① Thinking deep learning is a 2010s invention.
The ideas go back to the 1950s–60s (perceptron era). What changed recently is data volume + GPU compute — the ideas are old, the fuel is new.
② 'DL is just better ML.'
On tabular business data (churn, forecasting, recommenders), classical ML like XGBoost usually wins AND is explainable. DL dominates perception: images, audio, speech.
③ Defining DL by vibes instead of mechanism.
The definition: multi-layered neural networks mimicking the human brain, each layer learning more abstract representations. 'Modern' or 'advanced' is not a definition.
④ No engineering answer to 'why didn't you use DL?'.
The strong answer has three parts: data scale, explainability, measured lift over baseline — e.g. SVD recommender, +100.8% over popularity.
⑤ Confusing the ML/DL split with supervised/unsupervised.
Orthogonal axes: a CNN can be supervised or not; XGBoost is supervised classical ML. One axis is the TOOL, the other is the LABELS.
⑥ Assuming forecasting/recommenders need DL.
ARIMA, SVD, XGBoost are classical and production-proven (your own Adani ~20% lift). Reaching for DL by default is a sizing failure, not a skill signal.

📝 Notes

A short recap section that locks in the subset relationship before the course dives into algorithms.

  • ML recap: statistical tools that let systems analyze and visualize data, then predict and forecast (sales forecasting, weather, recommenders).
  • DL recap: the part of ML that mimics the human brain — a multi-layered neural network where each layer learns increasingly abstract representations.
  • History nugget: deep learning research is not new — it goes back to the 1950s–60s (perceptron era, 'mimic the thinking of the human brain'). What changed recently: data volume + GPU compute, which finally made deep networks practical.
  • Why the distinction matters in practice: DL needs big data + GPUs and gives state-of-the-art perception; classical ML often wins on tabular business data — which is exactly the data most companies (and your projects) run on.

💡 Interview angle: 'Why didn't you use deep learning for your recommender/churn model?' — Answer: classical ML was the right size for the data, it's explainable, and it beat the baseline by a measured margin. Tool choice is an engineering decision, not a fashion decision.
