📋 Coach's Review

Common learner gaps in this topic — check yourself against every line before the quiz:

  • Skipping standardization. Distance ADDS duration (minutes) to complaints (small counts) — the wider-range feature silently decides every neighbor. KNN is distance-based: always scale first.
  • k=1 memorizes. A single mislabeled or unusual neighbor flips the label (overfitting); k too large lets far-away, irrelevant points outvote the local neighborhood (over-smoothing).
  • Ties with even k. Binary classes + k=4 can vote 2-2 with no winner — prefer odd k, or define a tie-break rule.
  • "It trains on the data" misconception. KNN is a lazy learner: no training phase, all cost moves to prediction time — fine for 6 rows, a real scaling problem at 100K+ customers.
  • Reading vote share as a true probability. 2-of-3 neighbors is a crude confidence signal, and on imbalanced data the frequent class wins overlapping regions just by showing up more often.
  • Mixing up the metrics. Euclidean = straight line √(ΣΔ²), Manhattan = city block Σ|Δ| — and you should know which one your library uses by default.

(On real MyOperator data you'd standardize both features first; here the two axes have similar ranges, so raw distances are fair.)

✍️ Solve on Paper — KNN by Hand

Close the laptop. Pen and paper. The code works because this math works.

The recipe:

  1. Subtract the new point's coordinates from every training point, feature by feature.
  2. Square each difference (kills the minus signs).
  3. Sum the squares, take the square root — Euclidean distance.
  4. Sort ascending, keep the k nearest.
  5. Majority vote of their labels = prediction.

📝 Notes

The big idea
  • K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) classifies a new point by looking at the k closest training points and taking a majority vote.
  • Video example with k=5: among the 5 nearest neighbors, 3 are red and 2 are white → the new point is labeled red.
  • For regression, the same neighbor search is used, but the prediction is the average of the target values of those neighbors instead of a vote.
Measuring closest — distance formulas
Euclidean: √((x2−x1)² + (y2−y1)²)
Manhattan: |x2−x1| + |y2−y1|
  • Euclidean is the straight-line distance; Manhattan is the city-block distance. Both decide who counts as a neighbor, so the choice matters.
How a prediction actually runs
  • 1) Pick k. 2) Compute the distance from the new point to EVERY training point. 3) Sort ascending and keep the first k. 4) Classification → majority vote; regression → average of those k values. There is no formula being fitted — the neighbors are the model.
Choosing k — a hyperparameter
  • k is not learned from data — you set it. Practical recipe from the video: try k = 1, 2, 3 … up to about 50, measure the error for each, and keep the k with the lowest error.
  • k too small (e.g. k=1): a single noisy point decides → sensitive to outliers, overfits.
  • k too large: distant, unrelated points join the vote → boundaries get too smooth.
Known weaknesses
  • Outliers: a wrongly-labeled point sitting inside a neighborhood can flip the vote — KNN IS impacted by outliers.
  • Imbalanced data: a class with far more rows wins overlapping regions just by showing up more often.
  • Scale sensitivity: distances add features measured in different units, so the largest-scale feature dominates → standardize or normalize the data first.
Lazy learner
  • KNN has no real training phase: it just stores the dataset and computes distances at prediction time. All the computational cost moves to inference.
💡 Interview angle: three rapid-fire answers from this section. Is KNN impacted by outliers? Yes. Does KNN need normalization? Yes — it is distance-based. Do Decision Trees / Random Forests need normalization? No — threshold splits are scale-invariant.

🎯 Prove it with your projects

  • I can justify in one breath why distance-based models need scaling while the tree-family models in my reco engine do not — that contrast signals I understand the math, not just the API.
  • I tune k the disciplined way — sweep k = 1..50 and pick by validation error, never by gut feel.
  • Before fitting any distance-based model I standardize features and check class imbalance, because KNN silently hands overlapping regions to the majority class.
