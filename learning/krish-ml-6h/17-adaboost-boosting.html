① TE is a sum of WEIGHTS, not a count of errors. It only coincides with 'errors/n' at round 1 when all weights are equal; from round 2 on, TE = Σ weights of the misclassified.
② The α formula's details. α = ½·ln((1−TE)/TE): the ½ gets dropped, or the ratio flipped to TE/(1−TE) — both change every downstream number.
③ Direction of the update. CORRECT samples SHRINK (× e^−α); misclassified ones GROW (× e^+α). Learners often remember 'weights change' but swap the directions.
④ Normalization. After multiplying, every weight is divided by the new TOTAL so they sum back to 1 — skipping this breaks the bucket redraw.
⑤ How the next stump 'sees' the weights. Classic AdaBoost redraws the dataset WITH replacement through weight-proportional buckets — the hard points literally appear more often, rather than the stump reading weights directly.
⑥ The final vote is weighted. Stumps vote with strength α each (regression: weighted average) — not one-stump-one-vote. And TE > 0.5 makes α negative: the vote counts AGAINST the stump.

wrong   (C, E):   0.2 × e^(+0.2027) = 0.2 × 1.2247 = 0.2449 each

💡 Interview angle: why α = ½·ln((1−TE)/TE) makes sense — TE small → α large (an accurate stump gets a loud vote); TE = 0.5 → α = 0 (a coin-flip stump is silenced); TE > 0.5 → α negative (the vote counts against it).

✍️ Solve on Paper — AdaBoost, iteration 2

Close the laptop. Pen and paper. The code works because this math works.

The recipe (one full round):

  1. Carry the weights in: last round's normalized weights — check they sum to 1.
  2. Train the next stump (here on the OTHER feature) and write its rule.
  3. Total Error: TE = sum of the weights of the rows it MISCLASSIFIED.
  4. α = ½·ln((1 − TE)/TE) — the stump's voting strength.
  5. Update: correct rows × e^(−α), wrong rows × e^(+α).
  6. Normalize: divide every weight by the new total.
  7. Buckets: cumulative weights carve up the 0→1 line; the next redraw favours big buckets.

📝 Notes

AdaBoost = Adaptive Boosting

A sequential ensemble whose members are STUMPS. A stump is a decision tree of depth 1 — ONE feature, ONE split. That is a WEAK LEARNER: barely better than random guessing. AdaBoost chains many stumps into a STRONG LEARNER, and the 'adaptive' part is how it forces each new stump to focus on the samples the previous ones got wrong.

The pipeline, step by step
  1. Initialize: every sample gets the same weight = 1/n.
  2. First stump: choose the feature + split with the best information gain (lowest Gini).
  3. Total Error (TE): the sum of the weights of the MISCLASSIFIED samples — always between 0 and 1.
  4. Performance α (alpha) of the stump: computed from TE with the formula below.
  5. Update weights: correctly classified samples get multiplied by e^(−α) — they shrink; misclassified ones by e^(+α) — they grow.
  6. Normalize: divide every weight by the new total so all weights sum to 1.
  7. Buckets + resample: build weight-proportional buckets and redraw the dataset WITH replacement — previously MISCLASSIFIED points now sit in bigger buckets, so the next stump sees them more often and focuses on them.
TE = Σ (weights of misclassified samples) · 0 ≤ TE ≤ 1
α = ½ · ln((1 − TE) / TE) → video example: TE = 1/7 ⇒ α = ½·ln(6) ≈ 0.895
correct: w × e^(−α) ⇒ 1/7 · e^(−0.895) ≈ 0.05 · wrong: w × e^(+α) ⇒ ≈ 0.349 · then normalize (new total ≈ 0.649)
Final prediction

A weighted vote of ALL stumps. Classification: each stump votes with its α — sum the α per class; the biggest sum wins. Regression: weighted average. Not just the single best stump.

💡 Interview angle: why keep every stump instead of only the best one? Because each vote is weighted by its own α and the stumps specialize on different hard regions of the data — remove one and you lose the region it mastered.

Edge cases worth memorizing
  • TE = 0 → α → ∞: a perfect stump gets infinite say.
  • TE > 0.5 → α goes NEGATIVE: the stump is worse than a coin flip, so its vote effectively counts against it — better to flip its predictions.
