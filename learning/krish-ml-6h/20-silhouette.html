📋 Coach's Review

Common learner gaps in this topic — check yourself against each before the quiz:

① Wrong b(i).
b(i) is the average distance to the points of the nearest OTHER CLUSTER — not to the single nearest point, and not averaged over all clusters.
② Dropping the max(a, b) denominator.
s(i) = (b − a)/max(a, b) is a RATIO in [−1, +1]; writing just b − a breaks the scale and the sign interpretation.
③ Reading negative s as "outlier".
Negative s(i) means the point sits closer to a neighboring cluster than its own — it is probably ASSIGNED WRONG, not weird. The fix is a refit, not a deletion.
④ Trusting the average alone.
The video's K=2 topped the averages (0.704) yet lost to K=4, whose per-cluster bars were all positive AND matched the elbow. A high average can hide a pocket of negatives.
⑤ Assuming more clusters raise the score.
Adding K can lower the average silhouette — in the video's run the score fell 0.704 → 0.588 → 0.650 → 0.563 → 0.45 across K=2..6.
⑥ Forgetting the ≥2-members rule.
a(i) is undefined for a singleton cluster, so silhouette needs every cluster to hold at least 2 points.

Step 2 — a(i), b(i), s(i) per point: a = mean distance to own cluster's other points; b = mean distance to the other (nearest) cluster's points.

s(i) = (b(i) − a(i)) / max(a(i), b(i))

A: a = 1.4142, b = (7.0711+1.9799)/2 = 4.5255 → s = +0.6875
B: a = 1.4142, b = (5.6569+0.5657)/2 = 3.1113 → s = +0.5455
C: a = 5.0912, b = (7.0711+5.6569)/2 = 6.3640 → s = +0.2000
D: a = 5.0912, b = (1.9799+0.5657)/2 = 1.2728 → s = (1.2728−5.0912)/5.0912 = −0.7500 ⚠️

Step 3 — average: (0.6875 + 0.5455 + 0.2000 − 0.7500)/4 = 0.1707 — sklearn's silhouette_score returns exactly 0.1707.

Step 4 — interpret the negative: D's b (1.2728) is far SMALLER than its a (5.0912): D sits much closer to cluster 1's customers than to its own cluster-mate. It is almost certainly misassigned. Move D to cluster 1 and the average jumps 0.1707 → 0.5837 with no negative points left.

Business reading: D behaves like the passive segment but was assigned to the "growing" one — the growth campaign would waste budget on D while the passive campaign missed them. The negative silhouette is the audit alarm: refit before any campaign goes out. That is exactly why the video checks per-cluster bars, not just the average — a high mean (K=2's 0.704) can hide a pocket of negatives, and K=4 won on all-positive bars plus elbow agreement.

💡 Interview angle: 'A point has silhouette −0.3. What does that mean and what do you do?' → b < a: it fits a neighboring cluster better; treat it as a misassignment signal and re-examine K/init, not as an outlier to delete.

✍️ Solve on Paper — Silhouette by hand

Close the laptop. Pen and paper. The code works because this math works.

The recipe:

  1. Draw the points and their assigned clusters. Every cluster needs ≥ 2 points, or a(i) is undefined.
  2. For each point i compute a(i): the mean distance to the OTHER points of its OWN cluster (cohesion).
  3. Then b(i): the mean distance to the points of the nearest OTHER cluster (separation).
  4. Then s(i) = (b − a)/max(a, b) — do the division every time; the sign is the whole story.
  5. Average: per cluster for the bars, across all points for the headline score. Any negative = a misassignment alarm.

📝 Notes

Why it exists

You clustered without labels — so how do you know the clustering is any good? The silhouette score validates clustering quality WITHOUT labels. For each point it compares how well the point fits its own cluster against how close it sits to the next-nearest cluster, then averages.

The three quantities for a point i
  • a(i) — cohesion: the average distance from point i to the other points in its own cluster. Small a = the point fits in.
  • b(i) — separation: the average distance from point i to the points in the nearest other cluster. Large b = the point is far from the competition.
  • s(i) combines the two:
s(i) = (b(i) − a(i)) / max(a(i), b(i)) — range: −1 to +1
Reading the score
  • Toward +1: well placed — b much bigger than a means far from the neighbor cluster, snug at home.
  • Around 0: the point sits on a boundary between two clusters.
  • NEGATIVE: b smaller than a — the point is closer to another cluster than to its own, so it is probably assigned to the wrong cluster.
The worked example in the video (K = 2 to 6)

Average silhouette scores: K=2 → 0.704, K=3 → 0.588, K=4 → 0.650, K=5 → 0.563, K=6 → 0.45. So K=2 has the top average — but you do not stop at one number. You also plot the per-cluster silhouette bars (built from per-point values) and look for negative bars, which mark bad pockets of misassigned points. K=4 was chosen because its bars are all positive AND it matches the elbow method — two metrics agreeing beats one leaderboard number.

Code
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score, silhouette_samples
silhouette_score(X, labels) → one average score
silhouette_samples(X, labels) → per-point values for the per-cluster bars

💡 Interview angle: K=2 scored highest — why pick K=4? Because a high average can hide a cluster full of negative-scoring points; all-positive per-cluster bars plus agreement with the elbow is the stronger, more honest choice. This is the same validate-don't-eyeball discipline I use with HR@10 for recommenders.
