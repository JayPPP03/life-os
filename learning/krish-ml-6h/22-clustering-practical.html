📋 Coach's Review

Common learner gaps in this topic — check yourself against each before the quiz:

① Reading inertia's minimum as the answer.
inertia_ ALWAYS falls as K grows — below it went 148.0 → 4.0 → 3.0. You pick the ELBOW (the bend), never the lowest WCSS.
② Running KMeans bare.
Real runs pin init='k-means++', n_init and random_state — otherwise centroids (and segments) flake between runs.
③ Skipping the scale check.
make_blobs is already blob-shaped; real features are not. Different units → distances dominated by the wide-range feature → no blobs, garbage clusters.
④ Judging K by the average silhouette only.
Below, K=3's average (0.5158) hides that its power-user points collapsed to ≈0.17. Check silhouette_samples per point, like the video checked per-cluster bars.
⑤ Treating "prefer more clusters" as a law.
In the video, K=4 beat the higher-average K=2 on bars + elbow — a tie-breaker. Here the evidence is NOT tied: elbow AND silhouette both say K=2, so K=2 wins outright.
⑥ Confusing inertia with silhouette.
inertia_ = WCSS = compactness only (lower with any K). Silhouette adds separation and is comparable across K. They answer different questions.

🧮 Worked Mini-Project — 8 users, K=2 vs K=3: elbow, inertia by hand, silhouette decides

Business problem: 8 users of an app, scored on engagement and support tickets (make_blobs-style: two natural groups). How many campaign segments? Run the video's full chain — elbow → KMeans → hand-checked inertia → silhouette — on data small enough to verify every number.

UserEngagementTickets
U1-U41, 2, 1, 21, 1, 2, 2
U5-U87, 8, 7, 87, 7, 8, 8

Step 1 — elbow pass: inertia (= WCSS) for K = 1, 2, 3 → 148.0, 4.0, 3.0. The drop 148 → 4 is enormous; 4 → 3 is pocket change. The bend is at K=2.

Step 2 — fit KMeans K=2 (k-means++, n_init=10): every user lands with its own group; centroids = (1.5, 1.5) and (7.5, 7.5).

Step 3 — inertia BY HAND: each point sits exactly 0.5 off its centroid on BOTH axes:

inertia = Σ ‖xᵢ − c(label i)‖² = 8 × (0.5² + 0.5²) = 8 × 0.5 = 4.0 — matches sklearn's inertia_ exactly

Step 4 — silhouette, K=2 vs K=3: K=2 → 0.8659. K=3 → 0.5158: the extra cluster splits the power users into (7,7),(7,8) vs (8,7),(8,8), and per-point samples show those 4 collapsing to ≈0.17 — two micro-segments sitting on top of each other, with no distinct campaign value.

Step 5 — the choice: K=2. Note the honest difference from the video: there, K=4 beat the higher-average K=2 because the per-cluster bars and elbow agreed. Here the evidence is not tied — elbow AND silhouette both point to K=2 — so "prefer more clusters" never triggers.

Business reading: Segment 0 (1.5, 1.5) = passive users → activation campaign (onboarding nudges, first-success flows). Segment 1 (7.5, 7.5) = power users → upsell / advocacy campaign (premium tier, referral asks). Two evidence-backed segments, two messages.

💡 Interview angle: 'Why not just pick the K with the best silhouette?' → layer the evidence: elbow + average silhouette + per-point/per-cluster checks + business actionability. Three signals agreeing beats one leaderboard number.

In [4] — verify inertia by hand
# hand check: centroids (1.5,1.5) and (7.5,7.5); every point sits 0.5 off on EACH axis
sq = [(p - km2.cluster_centers_[l]) @ (p - km2.cluster_centers_[l]) for p, l in zip(X, km2.labels_)]

In [5] — silhouette: K=2 vs K=3, and the choice
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score, silhouette_samples
print("silhouette K=2:", round(float(silhouette_score(X, km2.labels_)), 4))
km3 = KMeans(n_clusters=3, init='k-means++', n_init=10, random_state=42).fit(X)
print("K=3 labels:", km3.labels_)
print("K=3 per-point silhouette:", np.round(silhouette_samples(X, km3.labels_), 4))

📝 Notes

The hands-on run of everything from the clustering sections: generate blob data, pick K by elbow, confirm K by silhouette.

  • Data: make_blobs(n_samples=…, centers=4) — synthetic data with 4 natural clusters, so the 'right answer' is known and methods can be judged against it.
  • Elbow pass: loop K = 1..10 fitting KMeans(init='k-means++', n_clusters=K), record inertia_ (= WCSS, within-cluster sum of squares). Plotting WCSS vs K shows a sharp bend at K=4 → the elbow method's answer.
  • Silhouette pass: compute silhouette_score for K = 2..6 → 0.704, 0.588, 0.650, 0.563, 0.45. Highest average is K=2 — but silhouette_samples shows which points are badly placed; K=4 has no negative per-cluster bars and matches the elbow.
  • Decision rule taught: when two K values are both viable, prefer more clusters (4 over 2) — finer segments generalize better to new data. Business meaning wins ties: 4 customer groups = 4 actionable campaigns.
WCSS = Σ distance²(point, its centroid)  → minimize by choosing K at the elbow, not the minimum WCSS (WCSS always falls as K grows)
  • 💡 Interview angle: 'Why not just pick the K with the best silhouette score?' → because a high AVERAGE can hide individual negative-silhouette clusters; you validate both the average AND the per-cluster distribution, then sanity-check against the elbow and business sense. Layering three pieces of evidence is the mature answer.
