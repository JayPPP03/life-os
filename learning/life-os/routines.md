# High-Performer Routines — Verified Edition (for Jay's Life OS)

Method: every item is traced to a primary source where possible (own books, own interviews, authorized biographies, Mason Currey's *Daily Rituals*). Items that circulate as internet folklore but lack a traceable primary source are explicitly flagged **[APOCRYPHAL / UNVERIFIED]** — do not build a life on those.

## Steve Jobs — Apple co-founder; ruthless prioritizer

- **Verified routine elements:**
  - Wore the same "personal uniform" (black Issey Miyake turtleneck, Levi's 501s, New Balance sneakers) to eliminate daily clothing decisions; he asked Miyake for "a lifetime supply." — *Isaacson, Steve Jobs (2011); Forbes (2022) on the Miyake origin*
  - Famous morning mirror question: "If today were the last day of my life, would I want to do what I am about to do today?" — asked for 33 years; if too many "no" days in a row, he changed something. — *Jobs, Stanford Commencement Address, 2005 (news.stanford.edu transcript)*
  - Annual "Top 100" strategy retreats: gathered the 100 most influential people, had them list priorities, then forced the list down to ~3 ("We can only do three") — focus meant saying no to good ideas. — *Isaacson (2011)*
  - Conducted serious conversations (interviews, strategy, hard talks) as long walking meetings around Palo Alto. — *Isaacson (2011)*
  - Flagged as unverified: the "he slept 10 hours / ate only fruit / microwaved lemons" type listicle details that float around. Not in Isaacson's book. **[APOCRYPHAL / UNVERIFIED]**
- **Source:** Walter Isaacson, *Steve Jobs* (Simon & Schuster, 2011); Steve Jobs, 2005 Stanford Commencement Address (verified transcript at news.stanford.edu)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Cut your to-do list to the 3 things that actually matter and let walking (not conference rooms) carry your deep conversations.

## Jeff Bezos — Amazon founder; "few high-quality decisions" operator

- **Verified routine elements (his own statements, 2018 Economic Club of Washington interview with David Rubenstein, reported by CNBC/Inc.):**
  - Protects 8 hours of sleep — refused to trade sleep for "more decisions": quality of judgment drops when tired. — *Economic Club of Washington, Sept 2018*
  - "I like to putter in the morning" — slow, unscheduled mornings: coffee, newspaper, time with kids; no meetings before 10 a.m. — *same interview; reconfirmed in 2025 coverage*
  - Schedules "high-IQ" (highest-stakes) meetings at 10 a.m., and makes only ~3 good decisions a day on purpose — "I don't make a hundred decisions a day"; nothing decision-heavy after lunch. — *same interview*
  - Flagged as unverified: precise daily schedules published by productivity blogs (wake times etc.). Bezos himself has never published a daily timetable. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Source:** Jeff Bezos interview at the Economic Club of Washington DC (Sept 2018), via CNBC and Inc. coverage of that interview
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Sleep first, decide fewer things, and put your best judgment window (late morning) on the highest-stakes call of the day.

## Roger Federer — tennis champion; recovery-as-training

- **Verified routine elements:**
  - Sleeps roughly 10-12 hours per day total — night sleep plus naps; his own casual line: "If I don't sleep 11 to 12 hours a day, it's not right" (from interviews/"Ask Roger Anything"-style sessions around 2015-2018). The exact hours vary between tellings; the 10-12h figure is his own repeated approximation, not lab data. **[VERIFIED AS HIS OWN STATEMENT, WITH VARIANCE]**
  - Practice philosophy: quality and precision of practice over raw volume — he trained noticeably fewer raw hours than rivals, prioritizing intensity, efficiency, and shot-making precision. — *Christopher Clarey, The Master: The Long Run... (2021, authorized-adjacent biography); Federer interview commentary*
  - Self-reports being naturally relaxed/calm between points, then fully switched-on — "talent is a myth" framing plus his masterclass in recovering from lost points. — *Federer, Dartmouth commencement address ("Greatest Point of All Time"), June 2024 (verified transcript)*
  - Flagged as unverified: precise practice-hour schedules, specific diet plans circulating in fitness blogs. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Source:** Federer interviews (2015-2018, incl. Australian Open week media); Christopher Clarey, *The Master* (Atria, 2021); Federer Dartmouth Commencement 2024 (dartmouth.edu transcript)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Recovery (sleep) is part of training, and perfect, focused reps beat grinding volume.

## Haruki Murakami — novelist; repetition as mesmerism

- **Verified routine elements (while writing a novel):**
  - Wakes at 4 a.m. and writes for 5-6 hours straight. — *Paris Review, The Art of Fiction No. 182 (2004)*
  - Afternoons: runs 10 km or swims 1,500 m (sometimes both, on his own later tellings); evenings free, in bed by 9 p.m. — *same interview*
  - Exact verified framing: "I keep to this routine every day without variation. The repetition itself becomes the important thing; it's a form of mesmerism. I mesmerize myself to reach a deeper state of mind." — *Paris Review (2004)*
  - Elaborates the same philosophy in his essay book: writing as physical labor — "physical strength is as necessary as artistic sensitivity." — *Novelist as a Vocation (Eng. ed. 2022; orig. Novelist as a Profession, 2015)*
  - Note: this ultra-strict mode is what he does when deep in a book — he has said he relaxes the schedule between books. **[VERIFIED NUANCE]**
- **Source:** "Haruki Murakami, The Art of Fiction No. 182," The Paris Review, issue 170 (Summer 2004); Murakami, *Novelist as a Vocation* (2022)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** The identical repeatable day is the trick — rhythm itself puts you in the deep-work state, so protect a fixed wake-write-train-sleep loop.

## Charles Darwin — naturalist; two 90-minute blocks and three walks

- **Verified routine elements (Down House, per Currey, citing Francis Darwin's *Life and Letters*):**
  - Rose ~7-7:30 a.m., short walk, breakfast; then 8:00-9:30 a.m. — his most serious work block (~90 min). — *Currey, Daily Rituals (2013), "Charles Darwin: Three Walks and a Nap"*
  - Read letters, then a second ~90-min work block (~10:30-12); total real work: roughly 4-4.5 hours per day. — *same*
  - Noon walk on the Sandwalk (his gravel "thinking path"; he counted laps/paces on it), lunch, rest on the sofa listening to Emma read, a nap, afternoon correspondence, a second Sandwalk lap, light work ~4 p.m. — *same*
  - Evenings: family, reading aloud, two games of backgammon ("even a win of two or three games was odious to remember"), early sleep. — *same*
- **Source:** Mason Currey, *Daily Rituals: How Artists Work* (Knopf, 2013), Darwin entry, citing Francis Darwin (ed.), *Life and Letters of Charles Darwin* (1887)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Two protected 90-minute deep blocks plus thinking walks can out-produce a 10-hour grind — count depth, not hours.

## Jocko Willink — Navy SEAL commander turned author/podcaster

- **Verified routine elements:**
  - Wakes at 4:30 a.m. essentially every day — he posts a daily photo of his watch as public accountability. — *Jocko's own social media (the daily watch posts); documented in Yahoo Finance/Business Insider interviews (2017)*
  - Goes straight from bed to a workout in his garage gym (weights, cardio, functional work), then plans the day and starts work before most people wake. — *same interviews; his book*
  - Philosophy, his own words: "Discipline equals freedom" — the early rise is enabled by going to bed on time; motivation is irrelevant, systems and standards are not. — *Jocko Willink, Discipline Equals Freedom (St. Martin's, 2017); Jocko Podcast; The Tim Ferriss Show*
  - He deliberately shows the trade-off: early rising costs early nights — no late-night leisure. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Source:** *Discipline Equals Freedom* (2017); Jocko Podcast; The Tim Ferriss Show (ep. with Willink); Yahoo Finance/Business Insider interviews (2017)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Win the first battle of the day before the world is awake — one controlled, non-negotiable action that sets the identity for everything after.

## Benjamin Franklin — founding father; daily intention + review loop

- **Verified routine elements (his own written "Plan of the Day," from his Autobiography):**
  - 5-7 a.m.: rise, wash, "address Powerful Goodness" (prayer/contemplation), plan the day's business and "take the resolution of the day," study, breakfast.
  - The Morning Question: **"What good shall I do this Day?"** — *Franklin, Autobiography*
  - 8 a.m.-12 p.m. work; noon: read accounts, dine; 2-6 p.m. work; 6-10 p.m.: put things in order, supper, music or conversation, "examination of the day."
  - The Evening Question: **"What good have I done to-Day?"** — *same*
  - Sleep 10 p.m.-5 a.m. Honest flag: Franklin himself admitted he did not always keep this ideal schedule (and was famously not an early riser in practice despite "early to bed, early to rise"). **[VERIFIED — INCLUDING HIS OWN ADMITTED FAILURE TO FOLLOW IT]**
- **Source:** Benjamin Franklin, *The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin* (his daily plan and the 13 virtues)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Bookend every day with two questions — one intention in the morning, one honest review at night — so reflection becomes an operating system, not an occasional mood.

## Albert Einstein — physicist; walks and thought experiments

- **Verified routine elements:**
  - Daily walks, most famously the walk home from the Institute for Advanced Study (Princeton) with Kurt Gödel — attested by colleagues (Ernst Straus's memoir "Reminiscences of Einstein"; Oskar Morgenstern's accounts). Walking was his default thinking mode going back to his youth. — *Straus (1979, in Holton ed., Albert Einstein: Historical and Cultural Perspectives); Isaacson, Einstein: His Life and Universe (2007)*
  - Thought experiments as his core deep-work method — he described, in his own *Autobiographical Notes* (1949), the age-16 "chasing a beam of light" experiment that seeded relativity. — *Einstein, Autobiographical Notes (1949)*
  - Violin breaks at home; famously no socks. — *Isaacson (2007)*
  - His Princeton-era schedule was deliberately loose/erratic rather than a fixed timetable — he protected long unstructured stretches for thought. — *Isaacson (2007)*
  - Flagged honestly: the viral "Einstein slept 10 hours a night plus naps" claim has NO solid primary source — it circulates via sleep-science pop articles and listicles; biographies don't document it. **[APOCRYPHAL / UNVERIFIED]** The "genius by long sleep" story in general should not be relied on.
- **Source:** Ernst Straus, "Reminiscences of Einstein" (1979); Walter Isaacson, *Einstein: His Life and Universe* (2007); Einstein, *Autobiographical Notes* (1949)
- **The one transferable principle for Jay:** Guard long, unhurried walks with no inputs — that is where your hardest thinking actually happens.

## Cross-cutting patterns (what repeats across ALL 10)

1. **Sleep is protected, not sacrificed.** Bezos (8h on purpose), Federer (10-12h incl. naps), Murakami (bed by 9 p.m. to hold a 4 a.m. start), Franklin (10 p.m.-5 a.m.), Kohli (strict early sleep). None of the verified sources show a "sleep is for the weak" high-performer — that meme is folklore.
2. **One protected deep block per day on the single most important thing.** Darwin's two 90-minute pre-noon sessions, Murakami's 5-6 dawn hours, Bezos's 10 a.m. high-IQ meetings and ~3 decisions/day, Jobs's list cut to 3. Depth over duration, front-loaded before afternoon energy fades.
3. **Daily movement used deliberately — as recovery or as thinking time.** Darwin's Sandwalk laps, Einstein's walks with Gödel, Jobs's walking meetings, Murakami's 10 km run, Jocko's 4:30 a.m. session, Kohli's and Federer's training-as-performance.

(Runners-up that also repeat: ruthless decision-minimization — Jobs's uniform, Bezos's few decisions; and a written daily intention/review loop — Franklin's two questions.)
